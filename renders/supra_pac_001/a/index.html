What is a Supra PAC?

A Super PAC converts anonymous money into political outcomes at a scale ordinary donors cannot answer. A Supra PAC is the answer: a legal, fully disclosed, community-funded vehicle that sits above the same rules and runs them in reverse.

We spend where they spend. We publish what they hide. Every dollar we raise is named, every dollar we deploy is announced on the day it is deployed, and every chain we unpick is released with its source documents. Fire with fire — in daylight.

How we
work

Navigate through sections

When a Super PAC reserves airtime against a candidate, that reservation is public. Within seventy-two hours our members vote on whether to match it, and at what share. If the vote carries, we buy into the same markets, at the same weight, in the same week — and we announce the amount before the first spot runs.

Median member contribution across all matched buys to date is $31. No single donor may exceed two per cent of any individual match.

Read the deployment rules →

Our Research Desk works backwards from the committee filing: registered agents, incorporation dates, shared addresses, treasurer overlap, deed and lien records, and the timing of transfers relative to reservation deadlines. Where a chain terminates in a sealed entity, we say so plainly rather than inferring.

Nothing is published on a single source. Every claim carries the document that supports it, and every document is mirrored so it cannot be withdrawn.

See an open dossier →

Findings go out as a full dossier — chain diagram, named principals, source filings — released to local newsrooms in the affected market forty-eight hours before general publication, so the reporting lands where the money was spent rather than where the press corps sits.

We publish corrections at the same prominence as the original finding. Three have been issued to date; all three remain linked from the dossiers they amend.

Request a briefing →

Money buys attention; it does not buy turnout. Every matched buy is paired with a field programme run by members in the district — canvasses, phone banks, and public readings of the dossier at community meetings, so the finding travels by voice as well as by broadcast.
